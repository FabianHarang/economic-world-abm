# 07. Credit, Housing, Construction, And Equity Channels

Milestone 4 extends the ABM beyond goods-market and supply-chain dynamics into household balance sheets and asset prices. The goal is not to forecast house prices or equity prices. The goal is to let policy-rate changes propagate through mortgage payments, collateral, construction demand, firm valuations, and household portfolios.

## Mortgage Pass-Through

Households may carry mortgage debt and have a household-specific variable mortgage share. Variable-rate debt reprices quickly with the policy-rate-linked mortgage rate. Fixed-rate debt reprices slowly through a fixed-rate stock variable.

This distinction matters for Norway-first calibration because rate changes can reach household cash flow faster when mortgage fixation is low.

## Housing And Construction

The housing price index responds to demand, mortgage costs, bank credit tightness, and construction supply. Construction output is taken from the synthetic construction sector and remains subject to labor, capital, and intermediate-input bottlenecks.

This creates a two-way channel: higher mortgage rates can lower housing demand and construction activity, while supply shortages can support housing prices even when demand weakens.

## Equity Valuation And Portfolios

Firms receive a stylized equity value based on cash, output, leverage, and discount-rate pressure. Households hold equity portfolios and rebalance slowly between deposits and risky holdings.

Equity changes enter household net worth and consumption through a bounded wealth effect. This keeps the mechanism visible without assuming that all households behave like financial traders.

## Collateral And Bank Credit

Households can draw modest credit against collateral headroom when bank credit conditions allow it. Banks track mortgage books, firm-loan books, capital, and credit tightness. Higher mortgage stress and weaker bank capital tighten credit, which feeds back into firm working-capital costs and household collateral access.

## Current Assumptions

All Milestone 4 values are transparent placeholders. Norway is prioritized because mortgage pass-through, household leverage, and housing sensitivity are central to the first research direction. EU / Euro area assumptions must be separate because fixed-rate mortgage prevalence, rental markets, bank structure, and household portfolio composition differ across member states.
