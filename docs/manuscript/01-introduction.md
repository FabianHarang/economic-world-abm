# 01. Introduction

Policy-rate changes can propagate through an economy through several channels that need not all push inflation in the same direction at the same time. Demand may contract, but firm financing costs may rise. Household mortgage payments may increase, but housing supply constraints may keep prices from adjusting smoothly. Downstream demand shocks may propagate upstream through suppliers, while upstream cost pressure may pass downstream into final prices.

This project builds an agent-based macroeconomic laboratory for examining those interactions. The model includes heterogeneous households, heterogeneous firms, private banks, a central bank policy path, employer-worker links, a production network, housing, construction, equity valuation, and portfolio choice.

The first empirical direction is Norway because mortgage-rate pass-through and household leverage are central to the transmission mechanism. EU / Euro area comparison follows as a separate context because fixed-rate prevalence, banking structure, labor institutions, and housing markets differ across member states.

## Research Question

How does the inflation-interest-rate relationship behave inside a large heterogeneous-agent ABM with households, firms, banks, labor-market links, and production networks?

The model is designed to avoid hard-coding a conclusion. It represents competing transmission channels and uses paired-seed counterfactuals, sensitivity sweeps, and later calibration to identify the assumptions under which disinflation is immediate, delayed, weak, or reversed.

## Scope

Milestone 8 is still a research scaffold. The browser companion is useful for mechanism inspection, while the offline engine is the path to larger experiments. No current run should be interpreted as an empirical estimate for Norway or the EU / Euro area.
